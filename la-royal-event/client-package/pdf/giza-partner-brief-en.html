1 · What the programme is

A game the monuments themselves referee

Guests arrive as an investigation party. They are given a field diary that belonged to a fictional archaeologist. Part of the diary has been rewritten by someone who never stood on the plateau, and the group has four hours to work out which pages are genuine.

The group is split into teams of five and moves in three flows of ten, each flow with its own guide. At six observation points the teams check a written claim against what they can actually see: how many openings there are in the north face of Khufu, how many subsidiary pyramids stand beside Khafre, which structures the causeway connects, and so on. Four of the six observations produce a number. Those four numbers open a combination lock at the closing session.

Two of the six observations deliberately produce no number at all. Teams discover this only at the end, and it is the point of the whole day: some claims can be counted, others only look convincing.

Please note, this matters for site approval
  • Nothing is hidden, buried, placed on, attached to or touched on any monument.
  • Guests observe from the standard visitor paths. They do not enter closed areas and do not go inside any structure as part of the game.
  • Every object guests handle is our own printed prop, carried in and carried out by our staff.
  • No costumed performers on the plateau. Our staff work in normal clothing.
  • The only staged element is the closing session, which takes place in a venue agreed in advance, not at a monument.

4 · Your guides on the day

Three guides, one per flow of ten

Your guides carry the history. Ours carry the game. The two run in a fixed order at every stop, and it only works if the order is kept.

StepWhoWhat happens
ObservationOur assistantReads the task. The team looks and counts.
DecisionThe teamThey commit to an answer and place their tokens.
CheckThe teamThey open a sealed envelope and see whether they were right.
HistoryYour guideNow the full explanation, in as much depth as the group wants.

The single rule for guides: the historical answer comes after the team has committed, never before. A guide who explains too early removes the reason the group is there. Everything else is normal guiding work, and the more knowledgeable the guide, the better the stop lands.

Station detail, the wording of each task and the answers are handed to your guides in person at the briefing the day before, together with the route walked on foot. This document stays at the operational level on purpose.

Please confirm these in writing

We print the game materials against these answers, so the numbers must be verified on site before printing. Nothing here is unusual for a private group, but each point changes the way the day is built.

Access and entry

Access windowWhat exactly your exclusive arrangement covers: which areas, which hours, how many guests, and whether other visitors are present during that window.
PermissionsWho issues the permission, what it allows, and whether it needs to name our company as the operating partner.
Entry procedureHow a party of 30 passes tickets and security as one group, and how long it realistically takes on the date.
Opening hoursPlateau opening hours on the date, so we can set the start time.
Standard ticketHow far the standard ticket reaches at the Khafre valley temple and the Sphinx terrace on that date, and whether the lower viewing area in front of the Sphinx is open.

Movement and comfort

Standing pointsWhere coaches can stand for each of the four blocks, and how long the transfer between them takes with 30 guests.
Internal transportWhether internal plateau transport is needed between blocks, and what it costs.
Shade and waterShade points on the route and where we can stop for water. We carry 1.5 litres per guest plus reserve.
ToiletsA toilet stop inside the 02:35 to 03:00 block.

Closing session

The venueSix tables for five, one central table for the lock box, and a wall or side table for a chart 100 by 70 cm. Fifty minutes, seated, out of the general visitor flow.
PermissionWritten confirmation that printed props may be placed on the tables and that a short presentation may be given.
AlternativesIf no suitable venue exists inside the plateau, please propose one within ten minutes' drive. The session cannot happen standing in the open.

Photography

Guest photographyOur staff take photographs on phones and print team sheets on board the coach. Please confirm this needs no separate permit.
Professional cameraIf the client orders a professional photographer, tell us what permit is required, its cost and its lead time.
